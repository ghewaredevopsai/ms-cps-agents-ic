Part 1 — Copilot Studio in 30 minutes

The one-sentence version

Microsoft Copilot Studio is where you build an agent: something with a job, its own documents, its own connections to your systems, and a published life of its own. Copilot answers your question. An agent does a piece of work, the same way every time, for everyone who uses it.

Where it sits relative to what you already use

WhatWhat it is good forWhere it stops
Microsoft 365 CopilotHelping you, right now, inside a document or a chat. Drafting, summarising, rewriting.It forgets. Nothing is repeatable, nothing is shareable, and it cannot reach a system nobody has connected.
Copilot StudioBuilding a thing: named, grounded in chosen documents, able to call your APIs, publishable to Teams, and identical for every user.It is a prototyping platform. Real limits on scale, latency and fine control — we cover these honestly on the day.
Code-first frameworksFull control, when you have outgrown the above.Out of scope for this workshop. It is where you graduate to, not where you start.

The five words you will hear all day

  • Agent — the thing you build. It has instructions, knowledge, and actions.
  • Knowledge — the documents and data sources it is allowed to read. Ours will be fund factsheets.
  • Action — a call out to a system: a REST API, a spreadsheet, a database, an approval.
  • Orchestration — the agent deciding for itself which knowledge to read and which actions to call, in what order.
  • Grounding — forcing every answer to come from a document or an action rather than from the model's general knowledge. This is the difference between a demo and something you would put near a client.

Part 2 — Prompting patterns for consulting work

This is the whole cheat-sheet. It is short on purpose, because prompting is the part of the day we spend the least time on — it has a ceiling, and everything interesting happens above it.

The five-part prompt

PartWhat it doesExample
RoleSets vocabulary and standard of care.You are an investment consultant preparing a quarterly fund review.
ContextNames the source and the date. Prevents the model reaching elsewhere.Use only the attached factsheet, data as of 30 June 2026.
TaskOne instruction, not four.Extract exactly these fields: …
FormatMakes the output usable by the next step rather than only by a human.Return a markdown table with three columns: field | value | source.
RefusalThe single highest-value line. Turns a gap into a flag instead of an invention.If a value is not present, write: not stated. Do not estimate or infer.

The four rules that matter in a numbers business

  1. Name the source, always. Ask for a source column or a citation on every figure. An unsourced number is not evidence, however confident it sounds.
  2. Give it permission to fail. Without an explicit refusal line, a model will fill a gap rather than report one — and a fabricated tracking error looks exactly like a real one.
  3. Decompose. Extract, then check, then write. A single prompt that does all three does none of them reliably.
  4. Never let it do arithmetic that matters. A total, an average or a currency conversion should come from a system. This rule is the spine of the afternoon.
The ceiling. Everything above makes a person faster at a keyboard. It does not put the result into a workflow, it does not run when nobody is watching, and it is not the same tomorrow. That gap is what the rest of the day is about — and it is why the request for this workshop was build agents integrated into workflows rather than get better at prompting.

Part 3 — Agent anatomy: the four boxes

Every agent in Copilot Studio is these four things. When something misbehaves on the day, the fix is always in one of these boxes, and knowing which one saves most of the debugging.

BoxWhat it holdsWhat goes wrong
Instructions The standing rules, in plain English. Tone, refusals, what it may never do. This is production code that happens to look like prose. Too vague to constrain anything, or edited and then tested in a conversation that started before the edit was saved.
Knowledge What it is allowed to read: uploaded files, SharePoint, Dataverse. General world knowledge left switched on, so the agent answers from memory when the document is silent.
Topics Scripted conversation paths for things that must go exactly one way. Used for everything, turning a flexible agent into a rigid phone menu.
Actions Calls out to the world: REST APIs, Excel, SQL, approvals. This is what makes it an agent rather than a search box. An action description too terse for the agent to know when to use it — far and away the most common cause of "it just ignored my API".
The mental model to arrive with: instructions are the standing orders, knowledge is what it may read, topics are the scripts, and actions are the hands. An agent that cannot do something is missing one of the four — and it is nearly always the fourth.

Part 4 — The capstone brief

This is the part worth reading even if you skip the rest, because you will get more out of the day if you arrive with your own version of it in mind.

The problem we are solving on screen

A fund review pack is produced today by a chain of manual steps: documents are gathered, figures are read off them and retyped into a workbook, numbers are checked by eye, calculations are done by hand, a generator is run over the result, and the deck is then tidied. Roughly four hours, six hand-offs, and every error survives to the next step.

What gets built

One agent — the Investment Report Agent — assembled live across the afternoon:

StageWhat the agent does
IngestReads fund factsheets and manager updates as knowledge.
ExtractTurns the unstructured document into a validated 25-field record, flagging anything absent or ambiguous instead of inventing it.
EnrichCalls an external market API, an internal legacy system, a spreadsheet and a database for the figures the document does not carry.
PlanProduces a slide plan in which every figure carries the source it came from, and anything unsourced is listed rather than shown.
RenderHands that plan to whatever builds the file — which, in your environment, is the report generator you already own and trust.
ApproveRaises an approval card in Teams. Nothing is client-ready until a person says so.
TriggerFinally, the whole chain runs on its own when a document arrives — nobody opens anything, and the first human involvement is a decision rather than a task.

The one thing to bring

Think of one process you personally run — a quarterly review, a manager screen, a client pack — and roughly how many steps it has, how long each takes, and how often it runs. You will use it in a ten-minute exercise in the morning, and it is what turns the afternoon from a product demo into a plan for your own week. Nothing needs to be written down beforehand.
Everything on the day runs on synthetic data. The fund, its figures, its holdings and its manager are invented for training. If you follow along on your own machine, keep it that way: no real client, fund or member data goes into any agent you build during the workshop, and never into a trial or personal tenant.
